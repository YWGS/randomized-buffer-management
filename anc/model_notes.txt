Model comparisons and finite certificates
A companion to "A 1.5422 Lower Bound for Randomized Buffer Management in Shared-Memory Switches"

Scope of the main formal theorem

SharedBuffer.PolicyBridge.sharpFromPolicies in PolicyBridge.lean proves the
uniform lower bound from the packet model for arbitrary finite arrival batches.
A fixed private seed turns a policy into a function of the observed arrival
prefix. Packet.PolicyLaw requires an empty initial state, per-slot shared
capacity, legal admission and eviction, and measurable retention decisions.
Packet.Competitive compares the coin-averaged online throughput with every
feasible offline trajectory on the same fixed input, allowing a constant
that depends on the switch and policy. The proof constructs the presampled
assignments and their actual online and offline runs, supplies the fields of
Cohorts.Experiment and derives the root bound. The supplementary statements
below use the additional model comparisons and constructions specified in
each section.

1. Transferring the fractional lower bound

Bochkov et al. (2019, Theorems 2 and 4) prove that an integer-capacity switch
has an integral offline optimum even when fractions are allowed, and that every
deterministic fractional online policy has competitive factor at least sqrt(2).
We explain how their result implies the same baseline for a randomized integer
policy A against fixed inputs. Both policies see the current batch, may evict
old packets, and send up to one unit from each queue, with compulsory service
when stock is present.

Fix any arrival prefix. At one output, let p be A's expected stock just after
the preceding service, m its expected retained stock immediately before the
current service, and y the expected amount sent in that service. Expectations
are over the private seed for this fixed prefix. With a new packets we have
m <= p+a, y <= m, and y <= 1. A deterministic fractional policy knows these
quantities from the prefix and the law of A. If F is its own prior post-service
stock, it retains f=min(m,F+a) and sends z=min(1,f). This rule respects the
available supply. Summing f<=m over outputs gives the buffer capacity bound.

Let L denote cumulative fractional deliveries minus A's expected cumulative
deliveries at this output through the preceding slot. Initially p=F=L=0.
Inductively assume 0 <= p-F <= L. The pre-service shortfall m-f is at most
p-F. The new post-service shortfall is (m-y)-(f-z). It is nonnegative: if
f<=1 the fractional queue empties, and if f>=1 then m-y >= f-1. This new
shortfall is at most L+z-y, the new lead in cumulative deliveries. Induction
and summation over outputs show that the fractional policy attains at least
A's expected throughput on every fixed input, including its drain. The
comparison preserves any input-independent additive allowance on a fixed
switch. The hard input for the deterministic fractional policy may therefore
be chosen using A's law and fixed before its private seed is drawn. The
long finite prefixes of the construction in Section 7 of Bochkov et al.
allow the throughput to grow on a fixed switch (see their Section 5.2 for
reuse of expired labels). The single-output induction step is checked in
LargeBuffer.lean, theorem fractionalStep. The construction of the full policy
and the application of the earlier theorem are the mathematical argument above.

2. Every sufficiently large integer capacity

Let r be the root in the manuscript and fix 1 <= rho < r. The finite-switch proposition in Section 3 and
the limiting argument give a strict finite witness on K outputs with capacity
B0. Write p=floor((B0-K)x0), O0=S+n p, C=n x1+E_m+1, U0=S+B0 C, and
Delta=O0-rho U0>0. The offline construction earns O0 on every permutation;
U0 bounds one cohort's expected online throughput after any prior history.

Write the new capacity as B=h B0+s, with 0<=s<B0. Keep the same labels and
group sizes, use last arrival times h t_i, and offer B packets per receiving
label in each slot through its last arrival. Suppose h>=1. Offline allocates
h p at each main expiration, divided as evenly as possible among its labels.
A group of size d that has aged h tau slots now retains exactly
h max(p-d tau,0) packets. At an event the total stock of current and older
expiring groups is therefore at most h(B0-K). At most K additional places
are needed for strict survivors. Between events the assigned stocks decrease.
The peak is at most h(B0-K)+K<=B, and the offline throughput is h O0.

The online policy has the full B places. In units of B, the lag thresholds
are (h B0/B)T_k <= T_k. Replacing the actual thresholds by T_k decreases
each positive part, so the paper's original summation bound still applies.
Thus the one-cohort online upper bound is h S+B C=h U0+s C. The difference
between the offline payoff and rho times this upper bound is at least
h Delta-rho s C. For all h>rho(B0-1)C/Delta it is positive for every s.
On this fixed switch, use independent presampled copies separated by B empty
slots. For N copies the expected difference is at least N times the single-copy
gap, even when the policy keeps its internal memory.
Averaging the fixed-input competitive inequality removes the additive
allowance by letting N tend to infinity. In particular, for each rho<r
arbitrarily close to r, the same K=K(rho) works for every sufficiently large
integer B. LargeBuffer.lean checks the lag comparison, the scaled residual,
the peak estimate, the online bound, and the Euclidean-division argument.
The application of these scaling lemmas to the time-stretched packet inputs
for every integer capacity is the written argument in this section.

3. A finite square switch with bounded input rate

In the model of Aiello, Kesselman and Mansour (2008, Section 2), each input
port receives at most one packet per slot and inputs are processed in a fixed
order. The following independent certificate exceeds 3/2 under this restriction.
Use K=10^36 active output labels, N=32K physical inputs and N physical outputs,
and B=10^41 buffer positions. Reserve 32 dedicated inputs for each active
label and leave the other outputs idle. A receiving label gets one packet
from each of its inputs every slot, including the last, in fixed input order.
Viewing an arrival phase and its following departure phase as a pair accommodates
the convention of service before arrivals, since the initial service is empty
and the final queues drain.

There are n=7000 main events and one terminal event. Take v=100 and m=56.
Set M_0=K and t_0=0. For i<n, let d_i=floor(M_i/v), M_(i+1)=M_i-d_i,
and let t_(i+1)-t_i be B/(v^2 d_i) rounded to the nearest integer (ties up),
with a minimum of one. The terminal group has d_n=M_n labels. Assign all
expiration dates by a uniform permutation before the policy runs. Define
S=sum_{i=0}^n d_i t_i. For this parameter set, put
    q=floor((B-K)*16693/10^6).
The main group target q_j equals q when q<=31 d_j t_j, and zero otherwise.
The terminal target is zero. The optional downward rescaling in the checker
does not activate for these parameters.

Offline balances q_j across its d_j labels. If one label is assigned H packets
at its final slot e, retain max(1,H-31(e-t)) before service in slots t<e,
retain H at t=e, and subsequently retain max(0,H-(t-e)). Before expiration
a queue can gain 31 net units per slot while sending one. The test on q_j
ensures H<=31 e for positive targets and hence reachability from an empty
start. Each label needs at most 32 new packets per slot to implement its trajectory;
sequential acceptance can reject each unwanted arrival. The unit baselines
cost at most K additional places. The extra stock for a block at any t is
bounded by max(q_j-31 d_j(e-t),0) before e and max(q_j-d_j(t-e),0) from e
on. Balancing gives these expressions because all labels lose the same
integer number of units. Between consecutive event dates each summand is the
positive part of an affine function and their sum is convex. A maximum on
that interval occurs at an endpoint. The program checks all event dates and
finds maximum envelope 99997944376421758025502269268171584565978 <= B-K.
Thus each intermediate inventory fits. Offline earns O=S+sum_j q_j.

For the online bound let Y_i be the expected number of transmissions from
group i beginning with its last arrival slot. Since M_i/d_i>=v, the hidden
identities give vY_i<=B. For an earlier group j, expected residual stock is
at least max(Y_j-d_j(t_i-t_j),0). Sum the capacity inequalities for the n
main dates, retaining up to m older groups per date. The right edge omits at
most m(m+1)/2 appearances of a Y_j, each at most B/v. Charging the terminal
group at most B packets gives
    U=S+B+[nB+A+B m(m+1)/(2v)]/(v+m),
    A=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} sum_{j=max(0,i-m)}^{i-1} d_j(t_i-t_j).
The 32-packet stream at each live output reveals no impending expiration.
The bound even permits the online policy to see the entire current batch,
so it also applies with sequential input observations. Independent repetitions
on this same switch remove a fixed additive allowance.

The middle case of check_bounded_arrivals.py gives the exact strict gap
    2O-3U = 1826156315885563170196081624242288642209537/52 > 0,
and O/U is approximately 1.5014170219. This is a separate bounded-ingress witness for
the threshold 3/2. Its capacity and ratio calculations are checked by Python
integer and rational arithmetic; the construction is justified above. The
unrestricted-batch theorem in the manuscript gives the constant r.
